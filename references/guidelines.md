# KOLHUB Proposal Guidelines

## 1. Strategic principles
A strong KOLHUB proposal should feel like a campaign system, not a content list.

Use this order of thinking:
1. Business problem
2. Audience truth
3. Campaign tension
4. Creator role
5. Platform behavior
6. Content mechanism
7. Conversion path
8. Budget logic

## 3. Missing information handling
Group gaps into:

### Critical
Information that affects proposal accuracy: objective, audience, budget, timeline, platform, deliverables, product USP, mandatory restrictions.

### Nice-to-have
Competitors, previous results, preferred KOL profile, product claims, brand tone, visual references, seasonality.

### Safe assumptions
Use practical Malaysia-market assumptions only when needed. Format:
- **Assumption:**
- **Rationale:**
- **Strategic implication:**

## 4. Strategy framework
Always define:
- **Strategic Objective:** What the campaign must achieve commercially.
- **Audience Insight:** What the audience believes, wants, doubts, or needs.
- **Core Tension:** The gap the campaign can solve.
- **Consumer Trigger:** What makes people stop, care, save, click, visit, or buy.
- **Messaging Direction:** The persuasive angle.
- **Desired Action:** The next behavior we want.

## 5. Campaign idea rules
A good campaign idea must be:
- Easy to understand
- Flexible across creators
- Native to short-form content
- Relevant to the audience
- Strong enough to anchor the whole proposal

Avoid:
- Generic “awareness campaign” language
- Corporate lines without human truth
- TVC ideas forced into TikTok or Reels
- Too many themes in one proposal

## 6. KOL strategy logic
Use tiers only when each has a job.

| Tier | Strategic role | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Mega / Macro | Noise, reach, authority | Launch moment, big visibility, hero content |
| Mid-tier | Storytelling, education, lifestyle fit | Detailed review, routine integration, POV content |
| Micro | Trust, relatability, niche community | Honest review, family / beauty / food / local proof |
| Nano / KOC | Volume, social proof, conversion reminders | Seeding, everyday usage, comments-to-action momentum |
| Expert | Credibility | Health, finance, baby, beauty claims, technical explanation |

## 8. Platform role
Do not write one-size-fits-all content.

### TikTok
Best for: hook, speed, trends, reactions, social proof, TikTok Shop, Spark Ads.
Use: POV, challenge, skit, first reaction, street interview, “come with me”, problem-solution, live selling clips.

### Instagram
Best for: aesthetic credibility, lifestyle storytelling, brand image, Story reminders.
Use: Reels, carousel, Story sequence, creator lifestyle integration, product routine.

### XiaoHongShu
Best for: detailed trust, searchability, premium review, Mandarin/Chinese-market depth.
Use: review notes, step-by-step guide, comparison, photo diary, ingredient or benefit breakdown.

### YouTube
Best for: long-form trust, family/lifestyle immersion, product explanation.
Use: integration, review segment, challenge episode, family vlog.

### Livestream / Social Commerce
Best for: conversion, urgency, demo, objection handling.
Use: creator live, brand live, affiliate push, bundle highlight, limited offer.

## 10. Funnel model
Default phase structure:

### Phase 1 — Noise / Awareness
Goal: create attention and first impression.
Best tactics: macro reach, challenge, teaser, first reaction, scroll-stopping content.

### Phase 2 — Validation / Trust
Goal: prove relevance and reduce hesitation.
Best tactics: review, tutorial, expert explanation, parent POV, product demo, comparison.

### Phase 3 — Push / Conversion
Goal: drive action.
Best tactics: CTA content, offer reminders, livestream, Spark Ads, affiliate push, “why now” message.

### Optional Phase 4 — Sustain / UGC
Goal: keep momentum after main launch.
Best tactics: KOC seeding, repostable UGC, community prompts, always-on reviews.

## 11. Budget logic
Budget should follow campaign roles, not random spending.

Possible allocation buckets:
- Hero KOL / Macro
- Mid-tier storytellers
- Micro trust builders
- KOC / seeding
- Paid media / Spark Ads / whitelisting
- Livestream support
- Production / samples / prizes
- Agency management if instructed

If exact budget is missing, give a percentage framework and state that final costing depends on selected creators, usage rights, deliverables, exclusivity, and campaign period.

Do not claim guaranteed sales, guaranteed virality, or guaranteed ROAS unless data is provided.

## 13. Brand research and co-branding
Use when producing proposal visuals, pitch decks, campaign visuals, or client-facing creative.

### Brand research
Identify:
- Brand personality
- Colour palette
- Logo usage
- Visual style
- Tone of voice
- Product hero assets

Sources may include official website, social media, media kit, press releases, product pages, and campaign visuals.

### Co-branding role
KOLHUB = strategic partner / agency.

Brand remains the hero. KOLHUB supports.

Recommended usage:
- Proposal cover: Brand Logo × KOLHUB Logo
- Proposal footer: small KOLHUB logo
- Closing slide: Powered by KOLHUB / Prepared by KOLHUB
- Campaign content: only include KOLHUB if relevant and subtle

Do not:
- Oversize KOLHUB logo
- Clash with brand colours
- Force dual branding into consumer-facing assets
- Distort logos

## 14. Slide / deck copy standard
When the user asks for slides, write concise slide-ready content.

Each slide should have:
- One clear message
- Strong title
- 3–5 key points maximum
- Visual suggestion if useful
- No paragraph-heavy slides

Suggested deck flow:
1. Cover
2. Executive Summary
3. Business Challenge
4. Audience Insight
5. Campaign Big Idea
6. Strategy Framework
7. Campaign Phases
8. KOL Architecture
9. Creator Archetypes
10. Content Playbook
11. Platform Strategy
12. Deliverables
13. Timeline
14. Budget Allocation
15. KPI Framework
16. Next Steps

## 15. Writing style
Use polished, tight proposal language.

Good:
- “Build credibility through creators who can demonstrate real usage, not just announce the product.”
- “Macro creators create the launch moment; Micro and KOC creators make the message believable.”
- “The campaign should move from attention to proof to action.”

Avoid:
- “In today’s fast-paced digital landscape…”
- “Leverage synergies…”
- “Maximize engagement” without explaining how
- “A holistic 360 campaign” unless the plan truly covers it
- Repeating the same objective in every section

## 16. Final quality filter
Before giving the user the final answer, remove:
- Duplicate sections
- Empty buzzwords
- Over-explaining
- Unsupported claims
- Too many campaign ideas
- Random KOL tiers
- Generic content examples
- Any point that does not help the proposal sell the strategy
